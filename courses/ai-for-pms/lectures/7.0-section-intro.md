## Cost, Latency and Unit Economics
lead: Section one taught you what a token is. This section is what happens when nobody priced one.

@narrate
Back in Section one, tokens were a mechanism: why your feature broke at
scale, why context windows have a ceiling. This section is the other
half of that idea, the half with a dollar sign in front of it.

By the end, you'll be able to build a real cost model per user, per
call, know when caching or a cheaper model actually helps and when it's
a distraction, and spot the margin trap before it shows up in a
finance review instead of a design doc.

@slide bullets
kicker: What's ahead
## Five ideas, then a unit economics model you'll actually use

1. How AI features actually get billed, and the number that surprises people
2. The token cost model, built for a real feature, not an estimate
3. Caching, batching, and routing, and which ones earn their complexity
4. The margin trap, and the latency budget that shapes whether users stay
5. Building your own unit economics model, end to end

@narrate
Five ideas, and they build toward the same kind of deliverable every
section in this course has: something you actually take into a room and
defend.

You'll learn exactly how billing works underneath the sticker price,
build a real cost model instead of a guess, and learn which of the
common cost-cutting tricks are worth the engineering effort and which
aren't. Then the margin trap, the specific way a feature quietly loses
money per user, and the latency budget that decides whether anyone
sticks around long enough to see the answer.

It closes with you building the full model yourself, for a feature
you'd actually ship.
