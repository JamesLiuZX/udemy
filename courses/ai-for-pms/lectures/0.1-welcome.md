# You're accountable for an AI feature you're not sure how to judge.
lead: That's the actual problem. This course fixes exactly that.

@narrate
If you're here, I'd guess something like this happened.

An AI feature landed on your roadmap. Maybe you asked for it, maybe it arrived
from above. And somewhere between the demo and the launch date you realised you
had no idea how to tell whether the thing actually works — and that everyone in
the room was quietly waiting for you to say.

That's not a gap in your intelligence. It's a gap in the toolkit. The methods you
were trained on assume software that behaves the same way twice, and this doesn't.

@narrate
Here's the concrete change.

Right now, the honest version of your status update is "it seems to work well".
By the end of this course you'll be saying "ninety-four percent pass rate on our
golden set, three known failure modes, here's the one I'd hold the launch for."

You'll settle quality arguments with a number everyone can go and look at. You'll
know what the feature costs per user before anyone writes code, not when the
invoice arrives. And you'll have surfaced the legal and safety issues at kickoff,
not in the week you were hoping to ship.

kicker: How this course is different
## Three things I've committed to

- **You build, not just watch** — eleven artifacts you'll use at work
- **No tool worship** — the frameworks outlive whichever model is winning
- **The trade-offs stay in** — including where this approach costs you

@narrate
Three commitments about how this course is built.

First, you build things. There are eleven downloadable artifacts, and they're real
work deliverables — a spec, an eval harness, a cost model, a risk register. You'll
finish with a portfolio, not notes.

Second, I've kept this deliberately light on specific tools. Tools churn every few
months. Judgement doesn't. What you learn here will still be true after the next
three model releases.

Third, I've left the trade-offs in. Where this approach is expensive, or slow, or
overkill, I say so. You're going to have to defend these ideas to a skeptical
engineer, and you can't do that from a sales pitch.
