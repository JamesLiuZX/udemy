## You can now design a retrieval system, not just call one "RAG" and hope.
lead: Nine lectures ago, retrieval was a word in a vendor deck. Here's the honest tally of what it is now.

@narrate
Nine lectures ago, this section opened with a benefits assistant that
answered a vacation-policy question instantly, confidently, and completely
wrong, because nobody had given it the actual document. Here's what you
can do about that now.

You've got the test from Section two's decision tree in your hands for
real: is this actually a missing-fact problem, not a reasoning one, before
you reach for retrieval at all. You know the seven pipeline stages, and
which ones a first version can honestly skip. You can chunk a document by
what it actually is, an FAQ pair, a policy clause, a table row, instead of
one default splitter applied everywhere. You can name a retrieval target
in hit rate and precision, and defend it the way Section three taught you
to defend a threshold. You know six specific ways a system that passed
testing still breaks in production, and that two of them, stale answers
and permission leaks, are the ones that actually end enterprise pilots.

That's not trivia. That's the difference between nodding along in an
architecture review and being the person who asks the question that
saves the launch.
