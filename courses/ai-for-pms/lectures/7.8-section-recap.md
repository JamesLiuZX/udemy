## You can now put a real number on what a feature costs, and say honestly when it stops paying for itself.
lead: Eight lectures ago, cost was a line item nobody on the product side could defend. Here's the honest tally of what changed.

@narrate
Eight lectures ago, this section opened with a number most product
people had never actually built themselves: what a single call to their
own feature costs, in real terms, not a vendor's marketing page.

You've got that number now, and everything built on top of it. You know
how billing actually works, tokens in and tokens out, and you've built
your own cost model instead of borrowing someone else's estimate. You
can tell which of three cost-saving levers, if any, actually fits your
traffic, instead of building all three because each one sounds
reasonable on its own. You've scored real candidates on your own golden
set before comparing their price, and you've found the exact tier where
a feature's margin quietly crosses from profitable to loss-making. And
you've got a latency budget set from what an interaction actually
needs, not a number borrowed from a different feature entirely.

@narrate
Two downloadable artifacts came out of this section, and the workshop

Use that page on something real before you move on. A pricing
conversation is coming for every AI feature that ships, and it goes
very differently when you walk in with a number you computed instead of
one you're defending on instinct.

features eventually has to answer to: risk, and exactly how much
oversight a feature needs before anyone lets it run unsupervised.
