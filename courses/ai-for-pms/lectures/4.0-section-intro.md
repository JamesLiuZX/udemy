# Evaluation
lead: The instrument the rest of the course depends on.

@narrate
Welcome to the section the whole course has been pointing at.

Back in the first exercise you scored a ticket summary and watched five careful
people land three points apart. That spread is the problem this section solves
for good. Everything before now told you why you need a measuring instrument.
These nine lectures are where you build one with your own hands.

When you leave, the question of whether a feature is ready will be a number you
can defend, not a meeting you have to survive.


@slide bullets
kicker: Section 4 · The route
## What you will build, in order

- **The raw materials**: a golden set of fifty examples, then a rubric (4.2, 4.3)
- **The shortcut**: letting a model do the scoring, and catching its lies (4.4)
- **The harness**: built twice, in a spreadsheet and in Python (4.5 to 4.7)
- **The reading**: significance, and honest interpretation (4.8, 4.9)

@narrate
Here is the shape of it.

Two lectures on ingredients: the examples worth testing against, and the
questions you score them with. One on when a machine can judge for you, and how
to check it. Then you assemble everything, first in a spreadsheet so every
moving part stays visible, then in about thirty lines of code.

The last two teach you to read results without deceiving yourself. Take them in
sequence. Each one stands on the one before it.
