## The Mental Model
lead: Enough mechanism to make good decisions, and no more.

@narrate
Section zero gave you the one idea: a distribution, not a function. This
section is where that idea gets a mechanism underneath it, just enough of
one.

Not a machine learning course. You will not train a model, and you don't
need to. You need the six or seven facts about how these things actually
work that keep you from making an expensive, avoidable mistake.

@slide bullets
kicker: What's ahead
## Six mechanisms, then the checklist that uses all of them

1. What the model is actually doing, and four consequences that follow
2. Tokens and context windows: where features break at scale
3. Temperature: why the same prompt answers differently
4. The capability frontier, and why hallucination isn't a bug
5. Seven feature archetypes, and the checklist for saying no

@narrate
Six mechanisms, roughly in the order they'll bite you.

You'll learn what the model is actually doing under the fluent sentences,
and four consequences that follow directly from it. Tokens and context
windows, because that's where a feature that worked in the demo quietly
breaks once real users show up. Temperature, so you can explain why the
same prompt gives two different answers without calling it a bug.

Then the honest state of what these models can and can't do, and why
hallucination is the mechanism working as designed rather than a defect.
It closes with seven archetypes of AI feature so you can name what you're
building, and a checklist for the question nobody wants to ask out loud:
should this be an AI feature at all.
