## Risk, Safety and Governance
lead: Section six gave you blast radius for one agent's actions. This section is the general version, for every way an AI feature can go wrong.

@narrate
Section six asked two questions about a single action an agent might
take: can it be undone, and what does it cost if it's wrong. This
section asks the same two questions about the feature as a whole, the
leaked document, the injected instruction, the quietly biased output,
before legal asks them for you.

By the end, you'll have a risk register that actually holds up in a
review, and you'll know exactly which regulations apply to your feature
and which ones are someone else's problem entirely.

## Six ideas, then a red-team workshop on your own feature

1. The AI risk register, scored the way 6.6 scored blast radius
2. Prompt injection, explained without the security-team jargon
3. PII, data residency, and who actually owns training-data rights
4. The EU AI Act, and what genuinely applies to you
5. Bias and fairness, scoped to your specific feature
6. Incident response, written before you need it, not during

@narrate
Six ideas, each one something legal or security will eventually ask you
about, whether or not you've prepared an answer.

You'll learn to register a risk the same rigorous way you already score
blast radius, read a regulation for what it actually requires instead of
what a headline implied, and scope a fairness question to your own
feature instead of a topic too broad to act on.

It closes with you red-teaming your own feature honestly, before someone
else does it for you in production.
