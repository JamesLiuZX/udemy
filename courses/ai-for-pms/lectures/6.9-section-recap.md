## You can now tell an agent from a workflow wearing the word, and scope the difference safely.
lead: Ten lectures ago, "agent" was the fourth box on a decision tree. Here's the honest tally of what it is now.

@narrate
Ten lectures ago, this section opened with the riskiest, most expensive
box on Section two's decision tree, the one worth reaching for last.
Here's what you can actually do with it now.

You've got the one-question test for whether something calling itself
an agent actually is one: who decides the next step, its builder in
advance or the model in the moment. You can compute the real cost of a
multi-step chain instead of trusting how reliable a demo felt, and you
know that a second agent adds a handoff, not a shortcut. You know the
three patterns that keep an agent alive past its first bad network
timeout, and the two questions, reversible and how much it costs if
wrong, that tell you exactly where a guardrail has to go. And you've got
a four-row checklist for the moments none of that is enough, where the
honest answer is to stay a workflow a while longer.
