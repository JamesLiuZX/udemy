## Doing the Job
lead: Nine sections gave you the skill. This one is about making it visible: in a sprint, a roadmap, an interview, and your first ninety days.

@narrate
Everything so far has been building a real capability: reading a
benchmark, building a golden set, scoping an agent, modelling cost,
running a risk register, measuring what matters. None of it counts for
much if it stays invisible, known only to you.

This section is about the visible half of the job: running a discovery
sprint, managing what stakeholders expect, building a roadmap that
doesn't quietly overpromise, and knowing when to build a tool yourself
instead of waiting for someone else to.

## Six ideas, then your first ninety days

1. Running an AI discovery sprint
2. Managing stakeholder AI expectations
3. The AI roadmap that doesn't lie
4. Vibe-coding your own tools: what it's for, and what it costs

@narrate
Six ideas, all about turning what you know into something someone
else can see: a sprint they sat in, a roadmap they trust, a portfolio
that proves the work instead of describing it.

You'll learn how to run the first two weeks with a new AI opportunity,
how to keep expectations honest without sounding like the person always
saying no, and when building your own small tool is the right call
instead of a shortcut you'll regret.

It closes with the twelve artifacts you've already built, packaged into
something you'd actually hand an interviewer, and a thirty-sixty-ninety
day plan for turning all of it into how you actually work.
