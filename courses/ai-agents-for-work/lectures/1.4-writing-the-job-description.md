## Three lectures, one page.

lead: Onboarding, the trust ladder, blast radius: they all live on this one document now.

@narrate
Everything in this section so far has been a habit or a principle.
This lecture turns it into a document, because a principle you cannot
point to is a principle you will skip under deadline pressure. The job
description is the artifact that makes the four onboarding habits from
1.1 impossible to shortcut, because you cannot fill it in without
answering them.


@slide bullets
kicker: Seven questions, in order
## What the template actually asks

1. The job, in one sentence, the way you'd describe it to another manager
2. What it can see: the exact folder, sheet or account, named specifically
3. What it can do: read only, draft only, or act
4. The ceiling: a number or condition it may never cross alone
5. What "urgent" or "correct" means in this specific job
6. The trial task, and what passing it looks like
7. Who signs off before it climbs a rung

@narrate
Notice question two asks for the exact folder, not "my email." Vague
scope is where blast radius quietly grows past what anyone intended.
Question four is the one people most often leave blank, and it is the
one that matters most: a ceiling only works if you wrote it down before
you needed it, not after.


@slide example
kicker: Worked example
::: example Priya's first draft, for the inbox triage agent
**The job**: flags any email that needs a same-day reply, in one sentence
a manager would recognise.
**Scope**: reads the Renewals inbox only. Writes nothing.
**Action**: read only. No drafts yet, that comes with Agent 4.
**Ceiling**: never marks anything as resolved. Flagging only.
**Urgent means**: a client names a deadline, or mentions a payment,
refund or lapsed cover.
**Trial task**: one week, every flag reviewed each morning.
**Sign off**: Priya, before the trial task, then Marcus at Review.
:::

@narrate
Read that back and notice how ordinary it sounds. That is the point. A
good job description is not clever, it is specific enough that someone
else in your office could read it and know exactly what the agent is
and is not allowed to do, without asking you.
