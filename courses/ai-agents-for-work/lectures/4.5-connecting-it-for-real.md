@slide statement

## Unpin the test data. Publish the form. Paste in a real transcript after your next call.

lead: The same honest handoff from Section 3, one build later.

@narrate
Open your form trigger, unpin the test data, and click Publish in the
top right of the canvas. That turns your Test URL into a real,
standing form: one you can bookmark and open after every client call
from here on. Nothing else about the build changes. The prompt, the
model, the ceiling from your job description, all of it stays exactly
as you tested it.

Then make the ritual real, because an agent that requires remembering
is an agent that stops being used in a fortnight. Put the form's URL
where the end of a call will actually meet it: a bookmark on the
browser bar, or a link in the calendar entry template for your
recurring calls. Priya's version takes under a minute: call ends,
transcript copied, pasted, submitted, and the action list is waiting
by the time she has refilled her water. The twenty minute write up
from lecture 4.1 is now a sixty second errand plus the checking, and
the checking is the part that was always worth a human.


@slide table
kicker: A short checklist before you rely on it
## Agent 2's trial, at a glance

| | This agent |
| --- | --- |
| Unit of evidence | One call, fully hand-checked |
| Trial length | Three or four calls |
| The check | Every line against your memory, side by side |
| Pass mark | Nothing invented, nothing missed, across all of them |
| Who else uses it | Nobody yet: see Section 10 before sharing the form |

@narrate
On the trial length, three or four calls is the whole trial, and the
smallness is deliberate: the unit of evidence is a call, each
one hand checked line by line, so four calls is four full audits. If
all four came back clean against your memory, you have better
evidence than a month of skimming would give you.

The last row matters more than it looks. A form is easy to share.
Section 10 is where sharing becomes a deliberate rollout instead of an
accident, and this is the moment worth remembering when you get there.

Why not just send the link around now, if colleagues would find it
useful? Because a shared form quietly changes what the system is.
Every transcript submitted through it runs on your model credential,
under the data terms you accepted, through the briefing you tuned for
your calls, and none of the people pasting transcripts in have read
any of that. What feels like generosity is actually them inheriting
your decisions invisibly. The rollout section exists to do that
handover properly: their own credentials, their own copy to tune, and
a trial of their own. Until then, the form stays yours, and "who else
might use this" stays a list on paper.


@slide two-col
kicker: Exporting what you built
## The same two-minute step as Section 3

::: cols
:: card good
### Export as a file
Workflow menu, Download. A JSON file anyone can import into their own
n8n and adapt.
:: card bad
### What doesn't travel with it
Your model credential. Whoever imports this connects their own key,
same as C01.
:::

@narrate
That consistency is deliberate. Every agent in this course exports the
same way, so once you have done it once, you have done it for all four.
It also means the file itself is genuinely safe to forward: nothing
inside it can leak into a colleague's account by accident, because
nothing sensitive was ever stored in it in the first place.

Safe on credentials, that is. The Section 3 caveat still applies, and
it applies more here: your briefing travels in the file, and this
briefing names Priya and describes her calls. Yours will name you and
describe yours. Glance through the prompt before forwarding, date the
filename, and file it next to C01's export in whatever deliberate
place you chose last section. Two templates in that folder now. By
the course's end there will be four, and that folder is the rollout.
