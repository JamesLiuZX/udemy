kicker: The question before any of this works
## Where does n8n actually live?

lead: On your machine, for free, or on n8n's servers, for a monthly fee.

@narrate
n8n is open source, and that one fact drives every choice in this
lecture. It means you can run it yourself, on your own computer, for as
long as you like, at no cost beyond electricity. It also means n8n sells
a hosted version for people who would rather not run a server. Both are
the same tool. The difference is who is responsible for keeping it
running.

What does "run it yourself" actually involve, though? Less than the
phrase suggests, and it is worth demystifying before you choose. Self-hosted n8n is one small program running quietly in the
background of your computer, and your browser is the whole interface:
you open a tab, and the canvas is there. Nothing else changes about
your machine. The honest cost is the one word inside that sentence:
your. Your agents run while your computer is on, and pause when it is
not. For a work laptop and office hours tasks, which is everything
Priya builds, that costs you nothing. The day you want an agent
working at three in the morning while the laptop sleeps in a drawer,
that is the day the hosted conversation genuinely starts, and not
before.

kicker: The honest comparison, priced
## Self-hosted versus n8n Cloud

| | Self-hosted (Community) | n8n Cloud, Starter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Free, forever | $20/month, billed annually |
| Executions (workflow runs) | Unlimited | 2,500 a month |
| Who maintains it | You | n8n |
| Setup time | About ten minutes | About two minutes |
| Best for | This course, and ongoing free use | Skipping setup, or a team that wants n8n to host it |

@narrate
Cloud's higher tiers exist for teams running thousands of workflow runs a
month. Section 10's rollout plan is where that conversation becomes
relevant. For everything in this course, self-hosted is not a compromise,
it is the better choice: unlimited runs, and nothing to renew.

Do the execution arithmetic before the cap looks generous. One run is
one pass through a workflow, and a triage agent runs once per email:
Priya's inbox alone burns roughly nine hundred runs a month before her
other three agents do anything. One busy agent, a third of the Starter
cap gone. That is the quiet trap in per-run pricing everywhere, not
just here: the agents that help most are the ones that run most, so
success is exactly what walks you into the ceiling. Unlimited runs on
the self-hosted side is not a nice-to-have for a course that ends with
four agents running daily. It is the difference between adding your
next agent freely and auditing your old ones for permission first.


@slide bullets
kicker: What we are using
## The route this course follows

1. Self-hosted, on your own machine, using n8n's own installer command
2. No credit card, no account with n8n required to start
3. If you would rather skip setup entirely, n8n Cloud's free trial covers the whole course

@narrate
Every screenshot in this course was captured against a self-hosted
instance, so what you see on screen matches what you will see on yours.
If your workplace already runs n8n on a server somewhere, ask whoever
manages it for an account instead of installing your own. Same tool,
same lectures.

What the ten minutes of setup actually contains: n8n's install page
gives you one command to paste into a terminal, and that command needs
one prerequisite the same page links to. That is the whole recipe, and
the reason this course does not walk it keystroke by keystroke is that
the page moves with each release and this video does not. Follow their
current page, not a screenshot of an old one. Two habits to adopt from
day one, both boring, both load bearing. First, n8n does not start
itself when your computer restarts, so opening it becomes part of
opening your working day, like the inbox. Second, updates are yours
now: once a month, before you add anything new, take the current
version. A tool nobody maintains is how workplace automation quietly
dies, and it is a five minute habit to avoid. Put a monthly reminder in
your calendar now, while this lecture is open, and the habit exists
before it has ever been needed. Future you does not get a say in
whether past you set that reminder, which is exactly why it works.


@slide sidenote
kicker: The screen you will land on
## One form, four fields

::: split
:: main
The first time n8n starts, it asks for an email, a name and a password
to create the owner account. That account is local to your install.
Nobody at n8n sees it, and nothing leaves your machine unless you connect
it to something.
:: aside Why this matters
This is the last "just trust me" moment in the setup. Everything after
this is a tool you can inspect, running on hardware you control.
:::

@narrate
One practical note about that form: the password it asks for is a new
one you are inventing for this install, not your email password or
anything you already use. It guards the door to your workflows on this
machine, and since those workflows will eventually hold saved
credentials to your real accounts, treat it like a work password, not
like a wifi code. If the form ever asks you something this lecture did
not mention, the version moved. Trust the form.

Fill it in, and you land on an empty canvas with one button: build a
workflow. You have seen that screen already, in Section 0. Before you
move on, run the thirty second victory lap: close the browser tab, open
a new one, and find your canvas again. Sounds trivial. It is the step
that proves to you the tool lives on your machine and not in a tab you
are afraid to lose, and everyone skips it and then messages someone in
a mild panic two days later. Next lecture, we connect the other half of
the toolbox: the AI model that does the actual reading and deciding.
