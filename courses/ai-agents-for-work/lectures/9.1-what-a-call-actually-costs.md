@slide statement
kicker: The number that shouldn't be possible
## A month of Agent 1, running against Priya's real inbox, costs a few cents. Not a few dollars. Cents.

lead: This surprises almost everyone the first time they see the real number.

@narrate
Gemini's Flash model, the one every agent in this course was built
against, prices out at roughly thirty cents per million words of input
and two dollars fifty per million words of output. Priya's forty one
daily emails do not come close to using a million of anything in a
month.


@slide table
kicker: The rough arithmetic, worked out
## One month of Agent 1

| | Amount | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emails read a day | 41 | |
| Working days a month | 22 | |
| Total calls a month | 902 | |
| Estimated monthly AI cost | | Well under $1 |

@narrate
That number is not a rounding trick. Small, short, structured tasks like
triage and extraction are exactly what these models are cheapest at.
The bill that actually adds up belongs to a different kind of workload
entirely: long documents, high volume, or a model far more capable than
the job needs.


@slide callout
kicker: Why the pricing story changes fast
::: callout This exact model may not exist by the time you build this
Gemini's 2.5 generation of models has an announced retirement date
later this year. Prices for whatever replaces it will differ, and
probably will have dropped, since that has been the trend every quarter
this course has been in production. Check your provider's current
pricing page before you build, not this slide.
:::

@narrate
This is exactly why the course keeps tool-specific facts like this one
quarantined to lectures like this, instead of scattered through every
build. The judgement spine survives a price change. This one number does
not need to.


@slide bullets
kicker: The number that actually matters more
## Cost was never the hard constraint

1. AI cost for all four of Priya's agents together: a handful of dollars a month
2. Her time reading every output during Trial: real, and worth counting honestly
3. The number worth optimising is hours saved, not cents spent

@narrate
Keep that ranking in mind for the rest of this section. Next lecture
turns it into a document you fill in with your own real numbers.
