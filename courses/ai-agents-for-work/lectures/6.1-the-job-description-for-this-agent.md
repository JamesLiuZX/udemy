@slide quote
kicker: The scene this agent fixes
> I meant to chase that. I just forgot, and now it's two days overdue.

attrib: Every client-facing job, at least once a quarter

@narrate
Priya's renewals do not fail because she is careless. They slip because
a hundred small tasks compete for the same Tuesday, and a follow up due
in eight days loses to whatever is due today. This agent's entire job is
noticing the eight-day ones before they become today's problem, and
handing you a draft instead of a reason to feel behind.

Why a draft, rather than a reminder? Because a reminder adds a task to
your day and a draft removes one. "Chase Brightwell" on a to-do list
still costs you the finding, the phrasing, the polite-but-firm
calibration, at the exact moment you are busiest. A draft sitting
ready costs you a read and a send. The difference sounds small and
compounds enormously: the chase that takes four seconds happens,
every time, and the chase that takes four minutes is the one that
slips. This agent does not make you more disciplined. It makes the
disciplined action nearly free, which works far better.


@slide example
kicker: This agent's job description
::: example Filled from the C05 template
**The job**: drafts a chase email for any open renewal within three
weeks of its date.
**Scope**: reads the renewal pipeline. Writes one Gmail draft per
client that needs one.
**Action**: draft only. Full stop. No send action exists anywhere in
this build.
**Ceiling**: never mentions a penalty, a threat, or the premium amount.
Polite, factual, nothing else.
**Trial task**: two weeks, every single draft read by Priya before it
is ever sent by hand.
:::

@narrate
Read the action line again, because it is written differently from the
first three job descriptions on purpose. It does not say "mostly draft"
or "draft unless." It says draft only, full stop, and the build in this
section is going to prove that sentence is true in your wiring, not
just on paper. Write your own version of it before you open n8n.

Two other lines have grown teeth since the last build. The scope line
says this agent writes drafts into a real mailbox, and that is a
threshold worth saying out loud: for the first time in this course, an
agent's output will live inside one of your actual work systems,
wearing your account's name. That is exactly why the trial is the
strictest yet, two weeks, every single draft read before anything is
sent by hand. And the ceiling bans the premium amount from every
draft, which is not squeamishness about money. A chase email gets
forwarded, and a number that was fine between Priya and Ravi reads
very differently when it lands in front of his finance director. Money
figures belong in conversations a person chose to have, not in nudges
a robot drafted. The polite chase works without the number, so the
number stays out.


@slide callout
kicker: Why this is the design centre, not an afterthought
::: callout Draft-only isn't a safety feature bolted on at the end
You are going to build this constraint into the very first version of
this agent, the same way you would build a lock into a door while you
are still framing the house. Section 8 tells you exactly what happens
when a constraint like this gets added after something already went
wrong instead of before.
:::

@narrate
The house-framing image is doing precise work. A lock added while
framing sits square in the door and nobody ever debates it. A lock
bolted on after a break-in arrives as a patch, under stress, argued
about, and trusted by nobody. Constraints on agents age exactly the
same way: draft-only built in on day one is simply what this agent
is, invisible and unresented, while draft-only imposed after an
incident reads as a demotion someone will eventually relax. Section
8's near miss shows the difference from the inside, on this exact
agent.

Next lecture starts the build, and the first piece is deciding which
renewals actually deserve a chase this week, using the same pipeline
sheet Agent 3 already taught you to read.
