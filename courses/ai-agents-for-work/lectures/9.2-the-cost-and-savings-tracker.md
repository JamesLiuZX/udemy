@slide callout
kicker: Turning an impression into a number
::: callout "It feels like it's saving me time" convinces nobody
A logged number, even a modest one, convinces almost everyone. This is
the document that makes the difference for you.
:::

@narrate
Open the C09 template. One row per agent: the task it replaces, the
time it used to take you, the time you spend checking it now, and the
AI cost straight from your own provider's dashboard, not a guess you
made up on the spot.

You already own most of the raw material, which is why this lands
here and not earlier. The trial tallies from your sticky notes, the
flags agreed with, the drafts sent as written, are the evidence side.
What C09 adds is the time dimension, and a cadence: fill it in weekly
for the first six weeks, while the numbers are still moving, then
quarterly once they settle. Priya's own six week log is where the
next lecture's total comes from, so what you are building here is
not an admin chore. It is the exact document that turns "trust me"
into a number.


@slide callout
kicker: The line most people skip, and shouldn't
::: callout Count the checking time, not just the doing time
An agent that saves you twenty minutes but costs you five minutes of
review saved you fifteen, not twenty. Write the honest number. A
savings figure nobody can poke a hole in is worth more than a bigger one
that collapses under one follow-up question.
:::

@narrate
This single habit is what separates a number you can defend in a
meeting from one that gets quietly challenged and undermines the whole
pitch. Five minutes of honesty now saves you an uncomfortable moment
later.

How do you actually measure checking time without turning your week
into a stopwatch exercise? One representative week is enough: for
seven days, jot the minutes each morning's review actually took,
then use that as the row's standing value until something changes.
And watch for the quiet cheat that inflates every tracker
eventually: once checking feels routine, people stop counting it,
and the saving silently grows on paper while nothing changed at the
desk. The checking time never goes to zero in this method, so it
never leaves the tracker either. If a future you finds a row where
it has, that row is fiction, and Section 7's rubric has a name for
what happens next.


@slide example
kicker: The sentence this tracker builds toward
::: example Write it in your own numbers
"These four agents save me roughly four hours a week, cleanly counted,
for under five dollars a month in AI costs, and every output is still
checked before it's trusted."
:::

@narrate
That sentence works because it names the saving, the cost, and the
safeguard in one breath. A number with no safeguard mentioned sounds
reckless to a careful manager. A safeguard with no number sounds like
you have not actually measured anything. Write your own version now,
while the habit of counting honestly is still fresh from filling in the
tracker itself.

The two words doing the most work are "cleanly counted", because
they answer the question a careful listener was about to ask. Every
manager has been shown a savings figure that evaporated under one
follow-up, and the phrase tells them, before they ask, that the
checking time is already subtracted and the soft benefits are not
smuggled in. You are not just reporting a number. You are reporting
which kind of person counted it, and that second signal is the one
that gets remembered.
