@slide callout
kicker: The last step, fourth and final time
::: callout Unpin the test data. Connect your real sheet and your real Gmail account.
Nothing about the draft-only guarantee changes when the data becomes
real.
:::

@narrate
Open your Sheets node and your Gmail node, unpin the test data on the
first, and connect real credentials to both. The Gmail connection asks
for the same kind of permission scope Section 1.3 taught you to check:
confirm it can create drafts, and do not grant it more than that if your
provider lets you choose.


@slide bullets
kicker: A short checklist, one last time
## The same rungs, the most caution of any agent yet

1. Run it once and read every drafted email as though a client will see it
2. Leave it on Trial for two full weeks, every draft checked, per your job description
3. Open Gmail yourself afterward and confirm the drafts landed as drafts, not sent mail

@narrate
That third check might feel redundant after everything you just built.
Do it anyway. Section 8's incident is a reminder that the gap between
"I configured it correctly" and "I confirmed it behaved correctly" is
exactly where trouble gets in.


@slide two-col
kicker: Exporting what you built
## The habit, fourth time round

::: cols
:: card good
### Export as a file
Workflow menu, Download. The same two-minute step as every agent before
this one.
:: card bad
### What doesn't travel with it
Your sheet credential and your Gmail connection. Whoever imports this
connects their own, scoped their own way.
:::

@narrate
By now you could probably do this without the reminder, and that is
exactly the point of building all four agents the same way.
