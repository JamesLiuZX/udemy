@narrate
This is the connection ceremony's fourth run, and the last one this
course walks with you. It should take about five minutes now, and if
some part of you is doing it from memory rather than from the
lecture, that is the course working exactly as intended.

Open your Sheets node and your Gmail node, unpin the test data on the
first, and connect real credentials to both. The Gmail connection asks
for the same kind of permission scope Section 1.3 taught you to check:
confirm it can create drafts, and do not grant it more than that if your
provider lets you choose.


@slide callout
kicker: Thirty seconds that matter
::: callout Read the consent screen like the contract it is
It lists, in plain words, what n8n will be allowed to do in your
account. This is where you check the list against the job

:::

@narrate
Those thirty seconds repay themselves for years, so spend them
slowly. Where the offered grant is broader than drafts, and
sometimes it is, you have two layers already working for you: the
wiring cannot send regardless, and the credential is one you created
for this purpose, named for it, per Section 2.2's habit, so it can be
revoked on its own from your Google security page the day you retire
the agent. Broad grants are a reason for the naming habit, not a
reason to panic. You know exactly which key opens what, and exactly
which one to take back.


@slide table
kicker: A short checklist, one last time
## Agent 4's trial, at a glance

| | This agent |
| --- | --- |
| First run | Watched, every draft read as though a client will see it |
| Trial length | Two full weeks, every single draft checked |
| The confirmation | Open Gmail yourself: drafts landed as drafts, not sent mail |
| The tallies | Sent as written, edited first, deleted: keep all three counts |

@narrate
The confirmation row might feel redundant after everything you just
built. Do it anyway, the first day and again at the end of week one,
because it costs one glance at a folder. Configuration is what you
intended. Behaviour is what happened. Section 8's incident is a reminder that the gap
between "I configured it correctly" and "I confirmed it behaved
correctly" is exactly where trouble gets in.

The tallies are the trial's actual instrument, so keep them honestly.
Sent as written is the agent earning trust. Edited first is tuning
material: every edit you make is a sentence that belongs in the
briefing, and folding your three commonest edits back in at the end
of week one is how the week two drafts get better. Deleted is the
important column: a deleted draft means the agent chased something it
should not have, and each one gets thirty seconds of why. And a fortnight of those three numbers answers the two questions
Section 7 is about to ask of every agent you own: are this agent's
mistakes the kind you catch, and are they getting rarer?


@slide two-col
kicker: Exporting what you built
## The habit, fourth time round

::: cols
:: card good
### Export as a file
Workflow menu, Download. The same two-minute step as every agent before
this one.
:: card bad
### What doesn't travel with it
Your sheet credential and your Gmail connection. Whoever imports this
connects their own, scoped their own way.
:::

@narrate
By now you could probably do this without the reminder, and that is
exactly the point of building all four agents the same way. Date the
filename, glance through the briefing for anything personal, and file
it with the other three. Worth knowing about this particular export:
the draft-only wiring travels inside it, so anyone you hand C04 to
inherits the guardrail along with the workflow. They can undo it,
nothing stops a determined importer choosing Send, but they would
have to do it deliberately, in the open, on their own canvas. A
template that ships with its restraint built in is quietly doing
Section 10's rollout work already: the safe version is the default
version, and defaults are what people actually run.

@narrate
Four agents built, and look at Priya's morning now, because it is
recognisably a different shape. The inbox is pre-sorted, with the one
email that matters flagged. The last call's action items are listed,
owners attached. Monday's report drafts itself before she sits down.
The chases wait in her drafts folder for a read and a click. Nothing
in that list happened because she became more disciplined. It
happened because the boring halves of four jobs got delegated, each
one on paper, each one on a leash. The building is done. Everything
from here is the part that actually separates people who keep the
hours they got back from people who lose them again in three months:
checking the work.
