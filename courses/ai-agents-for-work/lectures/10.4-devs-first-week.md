@slide statement
kicker: The first colleague to adopt the templates
## Dev Anand imported the meeting notes agent, ran his first trial call, and found a mistake Priya's trial never had a chance to catch.

lead: Not because the agent got worse. Because Dev's clients are different from Priya's.

@narrate
Dev runs Calloway & Finch's smaller accounts. His first call using
Agent 2 involved two people on the client side who happened to share a
first name. The agent's action item read: "Alex to send the updated
schedule." Two Alexes were on that call, and nothing in the output said
which one.

Dev was, for the record, doing everything right. Marcus had approved
the rollout, the template was Priya's proven copy, and Dev was on
day one of his own trial, checking every extracted item against his
memory of the call, exactly as C06 told him to. Which is the only
reason the two Alexes problem was a thirty second catch at his desk
instead of a wrong schedule chased with a client he was still
building trust with. Would you have caught it on a skim? Honestly,
maybe not: "Alex to send the updated schedule" reads as perfectly
complete unless you were in the room.


@slide two-col
kicker: A different kind of mistake
## Not invented. Just not specific enough to act on

::: cols
:: card bad
### What Priya's own trials already found
Section 7.3: a fact that changed underneath the agent, a moved row read
as true. Section 7.5: invention, a fact that was never actually given.
:: card good
### What Dev's trial found
Ambiguity: a fact that was given, correctly, but not specifically
enough to safely act on
:::

@narrate
This is a third failure mode, distinct from both of Priya's. Nothing
moved, the way Section 7.3's sheet moved, and nothing was invented, the
way Section 7.5 named. The agent read the transcript correctly. It just
did not know that "Alex" needed a surname to be useful, because nothing
told it two Alexes were in the room.

Here is the generalisation worth keeping, because it explains why
Priya's six weeks of clean trials could not have found this in a
hundred more. A failure mode is not a property of the agent alone.
It is a property of the agent paired with a particular world, and
Priya's world simply contains no colliding first names among her
regular attendees. Same template, different client list, different
exam, and your colleagues' desks are all different exams too. That is why "Priya already proved it" is a true statement
that guarantees nothing about Dev's desk, and why the trial has to
travel with the template, every single time, to every new world it
lands in.


@slide callout
kicker: Why this is exactly what should happen
::: callout A trial exists to find the failure mode specific to how you use it
Priya's own meeting-notes trial never produced this problem, because
none of her regular attendees share a first name. Dev's did, in his very
first week. That is
not the template failing. That is the trial doing precisely the job
Section 7.1 described it for.
:::

@narrate
If Dev had skipped his own trial because "Priya already proved this
works," he would have shipped that ambiguous action item straight to a
client relationship he was still building trust in. Picture the version
of this mistake waiting inside whatever template you hand a colleague
next, because it exists: some fact about their clients, their sheet,
their calendar that your world never taught your briefing to handle.
You cannot predict it, and you do not need to. You need them running
the same first-week discipline you ran, and the discipline will meet
it for you. That is what you are actually handing over in a rollout:
not four files, but the habit that makes four files safe on a desk
you know nothing about.


@slide bullets
kicker: What changed because of it
## The fix, and the habit it reinforced

1. Dev's system message now asks for a surname or a role whenever two people share a first name
2. The fix went into his copy of the template, not into everyone's automatically
3. C10's rollout checklist keeps "run your own trial" mandatory specifically because of this

@narrate
Look at the shape of the fix too, because it settles a question
every team rollout eventually argues about. Dev's surname rule went
into his copy only, and that is correct, not untidy. His briefing
now knows something true about his world that is false in Priya's,
where the extra instruction would be noise. Templates fork honestly
as they meet different desks, the exports from Section 3.6 were
built to make that cheap, and the thing that stays shared is not
the briefing text. It is the method: job description, trial, spot
checks, and a failure-mode name ready for whatever each new world
serves up first.

One template, two different first weeks, two different real failures
caught, and neither of them by luck. That is the whole argument for why Trial never becomes optional
for you, or for anyone you hand a template to, just because someone
else already proved the shape works.
