@slide definition
kicker: The document this section builds toward

::: definition Six short questions, filled in before, not after
This is the worksheet the incident in the next lecture did not get run
against.
:::

@narrate
Open the C08 template. It is deliberately short, the same design choice
as C06 and C07: something you can actually fill in before you connect
a credential, not a form substantial enough to skip when you are in a
hurry. Keep it open while you build anything new for the rest of this
course, and honestly, after it too.

One scale note before the questions: the worksheet runs per
capability, not per agent. An agent gaining two tools is two
worksheets, because each tool has its own reach and its own
narrower option, and bundling them into one form is how the smaller
of the two rides through unexamined on the bigger one's paperwork,
the way a minor clause rides through on a contract everyone read
for its headline number.


@slide bullets
kicker: What the worksheet actually asks
## Six questions, in order

1. What is actually being added, in one sentence
2. Does this change the scope: narrower, equal, or wider than before
3. What exact permission level does the new credential ask for, and is there a narrower option
4. Does the output need a human step, and can you name the exact node
5. The blast radius question, asked about the new piece specifically
6. Sign-off: four checkboxes, all of them, no exceptions

@narrate
Question two is where most trouble would get caught if people actually
asked it. "Wider" is not automatically wrong, but it means you are not
adding to a job description anymore, you are writing a new one, and it
deserves the same care as C05 did the first time.

The sign-off row's four checkboxes are the first four questions'
receipts: scope named, permission actually checked, human step
decided, blast radius answered. Nothing clever, and that is the
design. The worksheet does not know anything you do not know. It
just refuses to let any one of the four go unasked, and "all of
them, no exceptions" exists because the box people skip is always
the one that would have been interesting.


@slide example
kicker: Worked example, before you see how it went wrong
::: example Filling it in for "a tool that checks for a reply first"
**What's added**: a search tool so the chaser checks for a reply before drafting.
**Scope change**: wider, if the credential covers the whole mailbox
instead of one label.
**Permission level**: check for a narrower, label-scoped option before
accepting the default.
**Human step**: none needed, the chaser is still draft-only downstream.
**Blast radius**: could read content outside the Renewals label if
scoped too broadly. That answer alone should stop you before you connect it.
:::

@narrate
Read that last line again. The worksheet, filled in honestly, would have
caught the exact mistake the next lecture is about, before it happened.
That is not hindsight. That is what the worksheet is for.

And be precise about what "should stop you" means, because it is not
abandonment. It means the connect step waits while you go and find
the narrower option: the label-scoped grant, the restricted key, the
folder instead of the account. Usually that search takes ten
minutes and succeeds, and the addition proceeds with a radius that
matches its job. Occasionally no narrower option exists, and then
you are making a genuine decision, wide access or no capability,
with your eyes open, on paper, in advance. Both outcomes beat the
third one, which is the next lecture.

kicker: Before you move on
## What to do now

1. Fill in C08 for one capability you might add to any of your four agents
2. Answer the permission-level question by actually checking the provider's consent screen, not from memory

@narrate
Do the exercise with a real capability, not a hypothetical one, and
do it even if you have no intention of adding anything soon. The
worksheet's questions only become reflexes through your hands, and
ten minutes with a capability you might genuinely want is the
cheapest rehearsal you will ever get for the Thursday when you want
one urgently. Next lecture is the story this worksheet exists to
prevent, told in full, including the part where the guardrail
actually held.
