@slide example
kicker: What Marcus actually wanted to hear
::: example Not "I've been using AI"
A number, a safeguard, and one specific thing he was being asked to
approve. Enthusiasm convinces nobody who has ever been burned by a
vendor pitch, and you have almost certainly sat across from someone
who has.
:::

@narrate
Priya's conversation with Marcus took four minutes. Not because he was
easy to convince, but because she had already done the work Section 9
walked you through: a real number, honestly counted, and a clear answer
to what could go wrong. You have that same work sitting in your own
C09 tracker right now.


@slide two-col
kicker: Two ways to open this conversation
## Enthusiasm versus evidence

::: cols
:: card bad
### "You should see what AI can do now"
Sounds like a sales pitch. Invites the exact skepticism it's trying to
avoid.
:: card good
### "Here's what I built, what it saved, and what nearly went wrong"
Sounds like a status update from someone who already thought this
through. Because it is.
:::

@narrate
The second version works because it leads with the thing a careful
manager is actually listening for: evidence that you have already
considered the downside, not just the upside.


@slide callout
kicker: The question a careful manager always asks
::: callout "What happens when it gets something wrong?"
Priya had an answer ready, because Section 8 gave her one: it drafts,
it never sends, and she reads everything for two weeks before trusting
it unsupervised. A manager who hears that answer stops treating the
request as a risk and starts treating it as something already managed.
:::

@narrate
If you cannot answer this question about your own agents yet, that is
worth noticing before you pitch anyone. Go back to Section 1's job
description and Section 8's worksheet until you can, and only then book
the actual conversation.


@slide bullets
kicker: The structure that actually works
## Four parts, in order

1. What you built and for what task, in one sentence
2. What it saved, from your C09 tracker, honestly counted
3. What safeguards are already in place, named specifically
4. The one specific thing you are asking for

Next lecture turns this structure into the actual one-pager you hand
over, so you are not reconstructing it from memory in the meeting, on
the spot, with everyone watching.
