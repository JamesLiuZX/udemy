@slide statement
kicker: The question worth asking
## If it can read that one email, what else can it read?

lead: All of it. Every draft, every thread, every attachment.

@narrate
An agent connected to an inbox does not see a curated pile of the emails
you would be comfortable showing a stranger. It sees everything a
mailbox holds: client complaints, a colleague's badly worded joke, a
half finished resignation letter someone saved to drafts by accident.

That is not a flaw in the agent you just watched work. It is what
"connect your inbox" has always meant, for every tool that has ever asked
for that permission. The difference is that this one reads what it sees
and can act on it, and that is exactly the shift worth pausing on.

kicker: The other side of the ledger
## What the same read just caught

| The Brightwell renewal | |
| --- | --- |
| Cover | Commercial property and liability |
| Premium | $18,400 a year |
| Renewal date | 15 September |
| Ravi's query, before the agent | Unread for 6 days |

@narrate
Hold the doubt and the win in the same hand, because both are real.
The message the agent surfaced was not trivia. Brightwell Manufacturing
pays Calloway and Finch eighteen thousand four hundred dollars a year
for property and liability cover, their renewal lands on the fifteenth
of September, and the question Ravi needed answered had been invisible
for six days. A commercial policy that lapses does not send a warning.
On the renewal date, the stock in Brightwell's warehouse is simply no
longer insured, and the first person to discover that is usually the
one filing a claim.

So the agent that read everything also caught the thing that mattered
most on Priya's entire book that week. That is the honest shape of the
trade you are being asked to think about: real access, real reach, and
a real catch that no amount of Tuesday diligence was going to make.


@slide two-col
kicker: Two reactions to the same fact
## Which one are you having right now

::: cols
:: card bad
### The reaction that stalls people
"This is too risky, I should not automate anything that touches real
client data."
:: card good
### The reaction this course teaches
"This is exactly the amount of access I would think carefully about
giving a new hire. So I will."
:::

@narrate
Both reactions start from the same true observation. Only one of them
leads anywhere. Refusing to connect an agent to real data keeps you safe
and keeps you exactly as busy as you already are. The other path asks a
better question: what would I actually do before trusting a new person
with this inbox?

Name the costs on both sides, because both sides have one. The stalling
path costs you every hour the agent would have saved, forever, plus the
next Ravi. The connecting path costs you checking time for weeks, and it
hands you a risk you now own on purpose instead of by accident. This
course never pretends the second cost away. It just insists you compare
the two like an adult instead of flinching at one of them.


@slide bullets
kicker: What you would already do for a person
## The instinct you already have

1. You would not hand them every password on day one
2. You would tell them what "urgent" means in your specific job
3. You would check their work for the first few weeks, not forever
4. You would decide in advance what they are never allowed to do alone

@narrate
None of that is exotic management theory. It is what any competent
manager does automatically for a human hire, without writing it down.
The entire argument of this course is that the same four habits, applied
deliberately to an agent instead of assumed for a person, are what
separate someone who gets real hours back from someone cleaning up a
mess three weeks in.

Each of those four instincts becomes one of the artifacts you saw listed
in the last lecture. Not handing over every password becomes a one page
job description that says what the agent may touch. Explaining what
urgent means in your job becomes the plain English rules you write into
the agent itself. Checking their work becomes a trial with a checklist
and, later, a spot check habit that never fully retires. Deciding what
they never do alone becomes a worksheet you fill in before the agent
acts, not after. The course did not invent a method and dress it up as
management. It borrowed yours.


@slide quote
kicker: The frame for the rest of this course
> An agent is an employee, not an app. You do not install it. You onboard
> it.

attrib: The one idea, Section 1

@narrate
That sentence is doing real work, not just sounding tidy. An app is
something you configure once and trust by default. An employee is
something you give a job description, a trial period, and a trust that
grows only as it is earned. Every agent you build from here on gets
treated the second way.

Why the app framing fails here, specifically: an app does the same thing
every time, so testing it once tells you what it will do forever. An
agent judges, and judgement varies with the input it meets. The
thousandth email can confuse it in a way the first nine hundred and
ninety nine never did. You cannot test your way to certainty with
something that judges. You can only supervise it the way you would
supervise a person, which is why the employee frame is not a metaphor
in this course. It is the operating manual.


@slide bullets
kicker: Before you move on
## What to do now

1. Open the C01-preview file you imported and look at what it can reach: one inbox, nothing else
2. Write down, in one sentence, what you would want to check before trusting it unsupervised
3. Keep that sentence. Section 1 turns it into a proper job description

@narrate
You do not need an answer yet, just the sentence. For what it is worth,
Priya's habit is to double check anything new once, out loud, before she
trusts it, and her sentence came straight from that habit: before this
runs without me, I want to watch it be right about a full week of my
real inbox, and hear myself agree with every call. Yours will be
different, because your job is. The shape is what matters: a concrete
thing you would watch, for a concrete stretch, before the checking
stops.

Hold onto the doubt you are feeling right now. It is correct, it is
useful, and the next section is going to give it somewhere to go.
