This is the document Priya actually handed Marcus. Open the C10
template. Six short sections: what this is, the numbers from C09, the
safeguards named plainly, what you are asking for, what you are
explicitly not asking for, and, if colleagues are involved, what
they commit to as well.


@slide example
kicker: The section people most often skip, and shouldn't
::: example "What you are not asking for"
Priya's line: "I'm not asking to change how sign-off works. That
decision stays exactly where it is." One sentence, and it is doing more
work than the rest of the page combined.
:::

@narrate
Naming the boundary is what turns a request into something a careful
manager can approve quickly, because it closes off the worry they would
otherwise have to ask about themselves. You already know why this
matters: it is Section 9.4's never-automate list, restated for an
audience of one.

kicker: If your team is involved
## The checklist that keeps a rollout from becoming a shortcut

1. Each person runs their own Trial, even on a template that already works for you
2. Each person fills in C05 for their own scope, not a copy of yours
3. Spot checks continue for everyone, indefinitely, not just during the first week

@narrate
That first line matters more than it looks, and the next lecture tells
you exactly why: what happened the first week someone other than Priya
actually used one of these templates.
