## On Meta and TikTok, you interrupt someone scrolling. On YouTube, you often interrupt someone who already wants something.
lead: That single difference changes what a hook has to do here, before it changes anything about the format.

@narrate
Every platform so far in this section has been about earning attention
from someone who wasn't looking for you. YouTube is frequently the
opposite. Someone searched a question, or clicked a video about a problem
they already have, and your ad shows up inside that intent, not against
a wall of indifference.

That changes the job of a hook. On TikTok, the hook has to stop a scroll
that has no direction. On YouTube's in-stream placements, the hook often
has to survive a skip button instead, five seconds where a viewer who's
already mid-task decides whether you're relevant to what they actually
came here for.

Shorts breaks from that pattern entirely. It's YouTube's own vertical,
scrolling, sound-off-by-default feed, closer in grammar to Reels and
TikTok's For You page than to anything else on YouTube itself.

Hold both of those facts at once, because they pull in opposite
directions. In-stream rewards relevance to an intent someone already
has. Shorts rewards exactly the native, unframed grammar Section 5.2
just taught you. One platform, two audiences behaving nothing alike,
and neither one behaves like the platform's own name would suggest.

@slide table
kicker: Two genuinely different placements
## Same platform, two different jobs

| Placement | Behaviour | What it asks of your ad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In-stream, skippable | A skip button appears after a few seconds | Earn the rest of the watch inside that window, or lose it |
| Shorts | Vertical, scrolling, sound-off by default | The same native-grammar discipline as Section 5.2 |

@narrate
Two placements, two different disciplines, on one platform.

Skippable in-stream ads live or die on that early window. Whatever
convinces someone not to skip has to happen inside it, not after. Shorts
plays by TikTok's rules almost exactly: vertical frame, sound off by
default, a viewer who'll swipe past anything that reads as an ad before
it earns the swipe past that reflex.

Budget production time accordingly: in-stream earns a dedicated cut only
when you believe the intent argument, someone here already wants roughly
what you sell. Shorts is close to free once your TikTok cut exists.

Treating YouTube as one platform with one playbook is the mistake here.
It's really two placements borrowed from two different logics, wearing
the same brand name.

Why does the skip button matter more than it sounds like it should?
Because it hands the viewer an explicit exit that Meta and TikTok never
offer in the same way. Scrolling past an ad on those platforms takes the
same one-second flick as scrolling past anything else, an ambient
decision nobody registers as a choice. A skip button is a deliberate,
visible action: tap this, and the ad is gone. That's a higher bar to
clear, and it's exactly why the first five seconds carry so much weight
on this specific placement.

@slide code
kicker: What actually changes in the cut
## Harlan's ad, adjusted for the skip window

```text
IN-STREAM, SKIPPABLE
0-5s   The exact same hook, unchanged
       (the skip button appears here, this is the test)
5s+    Proof and CTA, only if the hook earned it

SHORTS
Same cut as the TikTok version from Section 5.2.
Native grammar, sound-off design, no separate edit needed.
```

@narrate
Notice what doesn't change here as much as what does. The hook itself
stays the same words, the same "hour eleven" line. What changes is what
happens right after it: on in-stream, everything past five seconds is
conditional on that hook actually working, because a skip button is
sitting right there the whole time daring a viewer to leave.

Shorts, by contrast, barely needs a separate cut at all. The same
discipline that made Harlan's ad work on TikTok, sound-off, native,
no framing that announces itself, transfers almost directly, because
Shorts was built to compete with exactly that kind of feed.

That's a real efficiency worth banking. Of everything in your angle map,
the version you cut for TikTok is very close to done for Shorts too,
same export, same captions, maybe a different on-screen handle. The
in-stream version is the one that actually costs you a separate editing
pass, because it's answering a different question than every other
placement in this section.

Think about what that separate pass actually needs to do differently.
It's not a shorter or longer version of the same hook. It's a version
that answers "why should I keep watching this specific ad" rather than
"why should I stop scrolling", a subtly different question that rewards
a more direct, less atmospheric opening line.

@slide callout
kicker: The honest question to ask

::: callout Be straight about this
Not every product benefits from intent-driven placement. ==Ask whether
anyone searches for what you sell before assuming YouTube's in-stream
placements are worth the extra cut==.
:::

@narrate
One honest question before you build a separate cut for this platform.

Harlan's socks aren't something people search for by name. Nobody's
typing "graduated compression socks for twelve-hour shifts" into YouTube
before they've heard of the brand. That doesn't rule YouTube out, Shorts
still works exactly like TikTok does, but it does mean the in-stream,
intent-driven placement is a weaker fit here than it would be for a
product people already know they want and are actively researching.

Ask that question honestly about whatever you're selling before you
spend a whole extra cut on a placement built around intent your audience
might not actually have yet.

There's a useful test here, borrowed straight from Section 2.2's
awareness ladder: does your product sit closer to solution-aware or
product-aware, where someone already knows roughly what they're looking
for, or closer to problem-unaware, where nobody's searching for the
answer yet because they haven't named the problem? The first case is
where in-stream earns its extra cut. The second is where that budget is
usually better spent on one more Shorts variant instead.
