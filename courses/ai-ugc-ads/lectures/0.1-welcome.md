# Dana generated four ad variants in an afternoon. She still didn't know the winner.
lead: That gap is the actual subject of this course.

@narrate
A few weeks ago, a founder named Dana Whitfield sat down with four ad variants
her media buyer had just generated for her sock brand, made in an afternoon
instead of a week-long shoot. She had to choose one to run first.

If that sounds familiar, you've probably already lived a version of it
yourself: a product typed into a generator, and ten minutes later more ad
creative than a whole shoot used to produce.

Here's the part nobody warns you about. Making the ads got cheap. Knowing which
one to run didn't. That second problem is what this course exists to solve.
You'll watch it happen to Dana later in this section, then feel it happen to
you.

## What changes for you

::: cols
:: card bad
### Before
- You pick the ad you personally like best
- "This one just feels right" decides the budget
- A loser runs for weeks before anyone admits it
- Every re-shoot costs real money and a week of waiting
:: card good
### After
- The account picks the winner, you read the result
- A kill rule decides in days, not weeks
- A new batch costs an afternoon, not a shoot
- You can defend every spend decision with a number
:::

@narrate
Here's the concrete change by the end of this course.

Right now, the honest version of how creative gets chosen is "I liked this one
best". By the end, you'll be running a small, disciplined test with a kill rule,
the line you wrote down in advance that says exactly when to stop paying for a
loser, and reading the outcome instead of guessing it.

You'll spend less on production and more on testing, which is the trade this
whole course makes. And you'll be able to explain, to a founder or a client,
exactly why one ad is getting the budget and three others got killed.

@slide bullets
kicker: How this course is built
## Three commitments

1. **You build the machine, not just watch ads get made.** Eleven artifacts you
   put to work this week
2. **No tool worship.** Tools rotate every quarter; the brief, the kill rule and
   the read stay yours
3. **The costs stay in.** Where AI creative still needs a human, and where
   testing needs real budget, I say so

@narrate
Three commitments about how this is built.

First, you build things. Eleven downloadable artifacts, and they're the actual
documents a real creative operation runs on: a brief, a testing architecture, a
scorecard. You leave with a system, not a highlight reel.

Second, this is deliberately light on any one tool. Whatever generator is best
this month won't be by the time you finish the course, so what you're learning
is the brief, the pre-flight check, and the kill rule underneath it. Those
outlive the tool.

Third, I'm leaving the costs in. This is not a "go viral" course, and it's not
a claim that AI replaces judgement about your customer. Where a human still
has to make the call, I'll tell you exactly where.
