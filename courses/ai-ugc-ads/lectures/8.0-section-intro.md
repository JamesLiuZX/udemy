## Reading Results
lead: Winner, loser, or noise, without fooling yourself.

@narrate
You built the instrument last section. This section is where you
actually read what it produces, and that turns out to be its own
separate skill, not something that happens automatically once your
numbers arrive.

Harlan's own test proves both halves of that. A real winner showed up
against seven ad sets that were mostly noise, and weeks later that same
winner went quietly tired. Both moments needed a decision, made
honestly, the same decision you'll need to make on your own account.

## Four lectures, one honest read

1. The metrics that actually matter, per objective
2. Winner, loser, or noise: the decision tree
3. Creative fatigue: detection and refresh cadence
4. Iterate or new angle: what the data actually says

@narrate
Four lectures, then a workshop scoring a live test using everything this
section teaches. By the end, you'll have a repeatable way to read a
result honestly, whether it's a clear win, a clear loss, or the kind of
ambiguous middle that tempts people into fooling themselves.
