kicker: Section 5 · What Sam found in Ads Manager
## Sam opened Harlan's Meta campaign for a routine check and found a headline he'd never written, paired with an image he'd never approved.
lead: Nobody on the team typed that combination. The platform did, and it never asked first.

@narrate
Nobody had touched that ad in days. Go back to Section zero-point-three
for a moment: the whole course opened on one claim, nobody can reliably
pick a winning ad by looking at it, including you. Testing finds winners.
Taste doesn't.

As of this recording, Meta and TikTok have built that exact belief into
their delivery systems. Meta's Advantage+ suite and TikTok's Smart+ don't
just run the test you set up. They generate their own variations of your
creative, headlines swapped, images cropped differently, text repositioned,
and quietly test those against each other too, often without a single
click from you.

That's not a footnote. It changes what "your ad" even means once it's
live.

Sit with that for a second. Why would you spend five sections building
your own testing discipline if the platform's already doing something
similar underneath you? Because "similar" isn't "the same". The
platform's automation optimises the pieces you hand it. It has no idea
which pieces are worth handing it in the first place, no view of your
claims boundary, and no concept of which angle actually deserves to be
in the batch at all. It's very good at finding a winner among your
inputs. It has no opinion about whether those inputs were any good.

kicker: What's actually automated now
## Two platforms, the same underlying move

| Platform | What it automates | What that means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meta Advantage+ | Combines your headlines, text and images; crops and touches up creative per placement | The version a viewer sees may not be the exact file you uploaded |
| TikTok Smart+ | Remixes your assets into new combinations; allocates spend to whichever performs best | Creative rotation happens automatically, not just audience rotation |

@narrate
Notice the shared move underneath two differently branded systems. Both
platforms take the raw materials you hand them, not just one finished
ad, and recombine them on your behalf, then let performance decide which
recombination survives.

That's the same discipline Section 3.6's workshop taught you to do by
hand: generate a real batch, not one hero asset, and let a test sort
winners from losers. The platforms have simply moved a version of that
step inside their own delivery systems, running underneath the testing
architecture you build yourself in Section seven.

Here's what actually changes for you. Your job used to stop at "which of
my twenty variants won". Now it includes a second layer: which
sub-variation the platform generated from your winner is actually
running, and whether it still says what you meant. Winning at the level
you control doesn't guarantee the exact thing a viewer sees is still the
ad you signed off on.

That doesn't shrink your job, it adds a layer underneath the one you
already had. You still decide which angles are worth generating. The
platform just took over one narrower decision, which wording combination
performs best.

@slide code
kicker: What you can still control
## Automation is toggled, not forced, as of this recording

```text
MOST ENHANCEMENTS: ON BY DEFAULT
Image expansion, text overlay placement, music addition,
headline and text combination testing.

YOUR CONTROL
Each enhancement toggles individually in Ads Manager,
at the ad level, not all-or-nothing.

WHAT STAYS ENTIRELY YOURS
The claims boundary. No automated system checks whether
a generated headline crosses into a claim Section 6.2's
review would have caught.
```

@narrate
Worth checking on your own account before you assume anything. Individual
enhancements toggle separately, ad by ad, not as one all-or-nothing
switch. That control exists. Most people never open the panel to use it.

The line that matters most sits at the bottom of this table, and it
doesn't move no matter how automated the rest gets. Nothing in Meta's or
TikTok's creative automation checks a generated headline against a claims
boundary. If Advantage+ combines your text in a way that drifts past what
Section 6.2 would sign off on, that drift ships exactly as fast as
everything else it's testing, and nobody catches it unless you're
checking.

What would that drift actually look like in practice? Picture Advantage+
pulling one phrase from your primary text and pairing it with a headline
written for a different angle entirely, a combination that, read
together, states something stronger than either piece did on its own.
Neither half is new copy you didn't write. The combination is, and
combinations are exactly what a claims review has never had to check
before this generation of automation existed.

Worth flagging precisely because it's easy to miss. A human copywriter
combining two lines would notice the overreach instinctively. An
optimisation system scoring purely on click-through has no reason to
notice anything. It isn't being careless. It was never asked the
question.

@slide callout
kicker: The honest limit, verified

::: callout Be straight about this
As of this recording, creative automation optimises for performance, not
for compliance. ==Review what a platform's automated system actually
generated, the same way you'd review a generated static, before you trust
it ran unattended==.
:::

@narrate
One honest limit before this goes any further, and it's the same one
that's run under this entire course.

A generation tool, whether it's the one making your statics or the one
now remixing your headlines inside an ad account, optimises for the
metric it's given. Performance, in this case, not truthfulness. It has no
model of Section two's claims boundary and no reason to check for one.
That checking is still entirely a human job, yours, on a cadence, not a
one-time setup step you configure and forget.

Practically, that means pulling the actual delivered creative report
periodically, not just your uploaded originals, and reading it the way
you'd read a generated static in Section 3.3: does this still say what
Dana would sign off on? Most weeks, the answer will be yes, because most
recombinations are harmless. The check exists for the week it isn't.
