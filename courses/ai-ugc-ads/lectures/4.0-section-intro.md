## The Video & UGC System
lead: Scripted, generated, assembled: video ads without a shoot.

@narrate
Everything Section three taught you about statics has a video equivalent,
plus one problem statics never had: a face that talks needs someone's
permission to exist.

This section starts with rights, not generation, on purpose. Harlan's
first avatar draft ran into exactly that problem before it ever reached a
test, and it's the kind of mistake you want to catch early. Catch it on
your own ad account with a live campaign attached, and it costs you far
more than five minutes of checking would have.

kicker: What's ahead
## Rights first, then the actual production

1. The AI UGC landscape: avatars, licensed actors, and what rights actually means
2. Scripting for fifteen and thirty seconds: hook, proof, CTA
3. Avatar video generation, walked through properly
4. Product-in-video: what a generator replaces, and what still needs a phone
5. Assembly: cuts, captions, and designing for sound off

@narrate
Five lectures, and the order matters. Rights and the avatar landscape
come first, because generating first and checking rights after is exactly
how Harlan's first draft got made. Scripting comes next, then the actual
generation, properly licensed this time. The section closes on assembly:
turning a generated clip into something that works with the sound off,
which is how most people actually watch it.
