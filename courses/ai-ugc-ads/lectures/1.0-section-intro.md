## The Economics That Changed
lead: Why zero-cost creative moves the moat from making ads to testing them.

@narrate
Section zero gave you the idea: winners are found, not picked. This section
is where that idea earns its numbers.

Before you touch a generator, you need to know why this is even worth doing.
What actually got cheap, what didn't, how many variants finding a real
winner takes, and the one place a human still beats the machine outright.
Get this section wrong and you'll either under-test, or waste a budget
proving something you could have worked out on paper.

## Five ideas, then the numbers you'll use for the rest of the course

1. What zero-cost creative changes, and what it flatly doesn't
2. Why ad performance is a power law, not a bell curve
3. The coin-flip problem, named properly this time
4. The volume maths: how many variants a real test needs

@narrate
Five lectures, building toward one number you'll carry into every section
after this.

You'll see exactly what got cheap and what stayed exactly as expensive as
it always was. You'll meet the actual shape of ad performance, which
explains why averages lie to you. Then the coin-flip problem gets a proper
name and a number, the volume maths turns "generate more" into an actual
target, and we close on the one honest exception: where a bigger production
budget still wins, and why that's not where Harlan's money should go yet.
