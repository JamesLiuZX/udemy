## A talking product ad, built without a shoot, and a rights mistake caught before it cost anything.
lead: Seven lectures, one licence-tier catch, one sound-off discipline, and one artifact.

@narrate
Seven lectures ago, this section opened with a claim: an avatar is the
first thing in this course that needs someone's permission to exist.
Here's the honest tally on what that actually meant in practice.

You've seen a real avatar draft get caught on exactly that point, a
licence tier that covered organic use but not paid social, fixed before a
dollar was spent. You know how to write a script to an actual word budget
instead of a guess, when to trust a generated product shot and when a
single filmed clip is the safer call, and how to cut and caption an ad so
it works with the sound off, because that's how most of your audience is
actually going to watch it. And you've built B04, a brief template that
carries every one of those checks into your next video before you
generate a frame.

That's the same lesson Section 3 taught for statics, showing up again in a
harder medium: the checking work is what makes the generation usable, not
the other way round.
