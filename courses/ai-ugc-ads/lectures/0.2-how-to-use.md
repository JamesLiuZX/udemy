## Skip a workshop lecture and you'll finish with opinions instead of a system.
lead: Here's exactly what you'll have built, and where each piece comes from.

@narrate
Quick housekeeping before we get into the actual idea.

Every section here ends in a workshop, and every workshop produces one artifact:
a real document you'll use on a real account, not a worksheet you file away. Skip
the workshops and you'll still learn the ideas. You just won't have anything to
run on Monday.

So here's the map: what you're building, and which lecture hands it to you.

@narrate
That's the full set, and notice the shape of it before you notice the details.

Two artifacts before you generate anything. Two while you're generating. One
that exists purely to stop a bad ad before it spends money. Two for running the
test itself, two for reading what it told you, and two for turning one test into
an ongoing operation.

Most people building this by hand skip straight to generation, because that's the
exciting part. The order above is deliberate, and it's the order that actually
works.

@slide bullets
kicker: How the sections build
## The path, section by section

1. **Sections 1-2**: why this works, then the brief that makes generation worth doing
2. **Sections 3-5**: statics, video, and per-platform tuning
3. **Sections 6-9**: the part most courses skip: pre-flight, testing, reading, scaling
4. **Sections 10-11**: turning the system into income, then one full sprint end to end

@narrate
Here's the shape across the whole course.

The first two sections are judgement: why AI creative changes the economics, and
the brief that decides whether generation is worth doing at all. The next three
are production: statics, video, and getting the same set to perform on different
platforms.

Then comes the stretch that separates this from a tool tutorial. Sections 6
through 9 are pre-flight, testing, reading results, and scaling what works. That's
where the actual skill lives, and it's the part almost nobody teaches, because it
isn't as fun to film as generation is.

@slide callout
kicker: One honest note
class: center

::: callout Be straight about this
Testing needs real, if small, ad spend. This course does not get you around that.
What it gets you is ==never spending on an ad that was never going to win==.
:::

@narrate
One honest note before you go further.

Nothing here removes the need to spend real money to find a winner. That part is
unavoidable, and any course that tells you otherwise is selling you something.

What the system in this course does is stop you spending on ads that were never
going to win in the first place. That's a smaller promise than "never spend
money", and it's the one that's actually true.
