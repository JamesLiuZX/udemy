# B11 · The Weekly Creative Sprint Runbook

Part of *AI UGC Video Ads*. Built in lecture 9.1. The companion file is
`B11-weekly-sprint.xlsx`: an empty workbook with a this-week sheet, a weekly log and
the runbook. Nothing in it is pre-filled.

A winning test is a snapshot. Nothing about it guarantees another one is ready next
week. This runbook turns Sections 2 to 8 into a repeating operation, so a refresh is
built calmly in advance and not in a rush the day a winner cracks.

---

## 1. Every week, same order

| Step | What you do | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Score every live ad set | B08 results scorecard |
| 2 | For each winner: still healthy, or refresh or retire? | Lecture 8.4, B09 watchlist |
| 3 | Generate only what steps 1 and 2 require | Sections 3 and 4, B10 cost model |
| 4 | Pre-flight everything new before it launches | B05 checklist |
| 5 | Launch what cleared, isolated per ad set. Loop to step 1 | B07 testing architecture |

The order is the point. Generating before scoring is guessing at what is needed.
Launching before pre-flight ships whatever was made, not what cleared. Each step's
output is the next step's correct input.

## 2. How to use this

1. **Name an owner for step 1 and step 4 before the first week.** If nobody owns
   Monday's scoring, it is the first thing to slip. (Who runs which day is lecture 9.3.)
2. **Fill in your own weekday mapping** if Monday to Friday does not match your week.
   Scoring comes first and launching comes last, whatever days you attach to them.
3. **Each week, list your live ad sets** on the This week sheet. For each one, set the
   verdict from B08, the decision, whether new creative is needed, whether pre-flight
   cleared, and whether it launched. The Status column tells you the next step.
4. **Make only what scoring called for.** Step 3 has no fixed quota. A sprint that makes
   a set number of new pieces every week is optimising for busyness.
5. **Log the week** on the Weekly log sheet, including whether you ran scoring.
6. **Keep the runbook where you will see it on a Monday morning.** A runbook in a folder
   nobody opens is worth as much as no runbook.

## 3. What the Status column catches

| Status | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Score first | The ad set has no verdict yet. Do step 1 |
| Decide | Scored, but no keep, refresh, new-angle or retire decision |
| Pre-flight before launch | Creative exists but has not cleared B05 |
| Ready: launch isolated | Cleared. Launch it in its own ad set |
| STOP: launched without pre-flight | Pre-flight was skipped. Pause it and run B05 now |
| Check | A refresh or new angle was chosen but no creative is planned |
| Done | Nothing more this week |

## 4. Quiet weeks

Some weeks scoring turns up nothing that needs a refresh, and Wednesday to Friday are
genuinely quiet. That is the sprint working. The workbook says "Quiet week: nothing to
make. The check was the work."

Do not skip the scoring step because last week was quiet. Do that a few times and the
sprint becomes something you only do when a problem is already visible, which is the
reactive posture this section exists to prevent. The Weekly log counts weeks you
skipped scoring.

## 5. An honest test

At the end of a quiet week, ask: did you run the check, or did you feel confident
enough to assume the answer? Only one of those is the habit. The log should show a Yes
for scoring on every week, including the dull ones.

## 6. Open gaps

- The days are yours. The course fixes the order, not the weekdays.
- The runbook assumes someone owns each step. If you work solo, one name appears twice,
  and the pre-flight step is the one most worth handing to a second pair of eyes.
- It does not set a budget, a cadence for new angles or a number of ad sets. Those come
  from B07 and your margin.
