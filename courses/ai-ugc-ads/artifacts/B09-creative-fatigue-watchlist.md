# B09 · Creative Fatigue Watchlist and Refresh Cadence

Part of *AI UGC Video Ads*. Built in lecture 8.3. The companion file is
`B09-fatigue-watchlist.xlsx`: an empty workbook with a watchlist sheet and a
one-page refresh cadence. Nothing in it is pre-filled.

A winner does not fail. It wears out. On a dashboard the two look the same, and
they need opposite responses: a loser is killed, a fatigued winner is refreshed.

---

## 1. How to use this

1. **Set three cells before you look at any ad set:** the frequency at which you
   treat a winner as tiring, the fall in click-through rate from peak that counts
   as falling, and your weekly check day. Use the same tiring-frequency number you
   wrote in the results scorecard (B08). Decide now, not while looking at a tired ad.
2. **Add every ad set that has won** (verdict from B08). Record its peak click-through
   rate and peak frequency from the test that made it a winner.
3. **Once a week, on your check day,** enter current frequency and current
   click-through rate for each. Frequency is on the same dashboard as the click-through
   rate. It is the column you were probably skimming past.
4. **Read the Verdict column and act only on the pairing.**

| Frequency | Performance | Verdict | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Past your line | Falling past your line | Fatigued | Refresh the creative, keep the angle |
| Past your line | Holding | Watch | Do nothing yet. A small rise is normal |
| Below your line | Falling | Not fatigue | Investigate. This is a different problem (lectures 7.2 and 8.4) |
| Below your line | Holding | Healthy | Keep running |

5. **A fatigued row with no refresh ready is the urgent one.** Brief the refresh the
   same day. Mark "Refresh cut ready" Yes only when a cut exists, not when one is planned.
6. **Write the outcome to the asset library (B06)** with its date.

## 2. What a refresh changes, and what it does not

| Change | Keep |
| --- | --- |
| The hook | The angle |
| The pace | The claim |
| The actor reading the script | The underlying script |

The audience needs a reason to look again. The argument does not need to change.
The refreshed cut goes back through the same testing structure from B07 and earns
its own verdict in B08. It is not launched on faith.

## 3. Refresh cadence

| Step | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly | Update frequency and click-through rate for every winner |
| On a Fatigued verdict | Brief and make the refresh cut, same angle |
| Before launch | Test the refresh through B07, score it in B08 |
| Decide | Refresh or new angle, by the B08 next-action rules (lecture 8.4) |
| Standing | Keep one refresh brief ready before the next winner goes tired (lecture 9.1) |

## 4. Two mistakes this sheet is built to stop

- **Refreshing at the first tick.** A small rise in frequency is normal while an ad
  set keeps running. Reflexive refreshing spends production effort on a creative that
  was still earning. It is the same impatience lecture 7.4 warned against.
- **Waiting for click-through to confirm it.** By then you have paid tired-ad prices
  for days. Frequency climbs before performance cracks, so the watchlist watches both.

## 5. An honest test

You have used this well if, after four weeks, you can answer from the sheet alone:
which winners are tiring, which already have a refresh cut ready, and which row you
last checked and when. If you cannot answer the third, the habit is not yet a habit.

## 6. Open gaps

- The tiring-frequency line and the falling-performance line are yours. The course
  gives one worked example and no universal number, and this file does not invent one.
- Frequency is not labelled identically on every platform. Use whatever your platform
  calls the average number of times a person has seen the ad.
