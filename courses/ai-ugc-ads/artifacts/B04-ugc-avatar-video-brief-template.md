# B04 · The UGC / Avatar Video Brief Template

Part of *AI UGC Video Ads*. Built in lecture 4.6, the Section 4 workshop, from
lectures 4.1 to 4.5. Use it to brief and finish three video ads for the same
product you briefed in B01, to a checklist. Three ads, not one, because one video
tells you nothing about which angle wins. Three gives you a real spread to test.

The point of this workshop is not your favourite cut. It is a real spread you can
actually test. Three ads finished to a checklist beat one ad finished to a feeling.

---

## 1. How to use this

1. **Use the same product and brief as B01,** and pick three angles from your angle
   map (and the hook patterns from B02). Scripts come from them.
2. **Work in this order,** and expect more than the time shown if you are new to the
   tools:

| Step | Time | What "done" looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Write and time three scripts | 25 min | Each one read aloud, inside its word budget |
| Confirm the avatar licence tier | 5 min | Commercial use verified, not assumed |
| Generate three avatar clips | 15 min | Each watched in full, not judged from a thumbnail |
| Film one product clip | 10 min | One real ten-second shot, reused across all three |
| Assemble and caption all three | 25 min | Captions exact, muted-watch test passed on each |

3. **Do not take the two shortcuts.** Skipping the licence-tier check because the
   first draft looks fine is the exact gap that caught the course's first avatar
   draft. Skipping the muted-watch test because the cut sounds right with audio on
   leaves an ad that fails the one condition most viewers watch under.
4. **Rights and disclosure first.** The licence check comes before the script, before
   anything. Disclosure of synthetic media is built in Section 6.3 and added at the
   assembly stage; rights and faces are Section 6.4's checklist. This template
   records what you did. It does not tell you what is lawful or required where you
   advertise.

| # | Step | The rule |

| 2 | Script | Hook, proof, CTA, timed aloud before anything is generated |

## 3. Step 1 and 2: format and script (lecture 4.2)

A shorter script is not a trimmed long one. It is a different structure built for a
different job.

| Format | Word budget (natural spoken pace) | What it can hold | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 seconds | About 35 to 40 words | One hook, one proof point, one CTA. No room for an objection | A cold, unaware audience |
| 30 seconds | About 75 to 85 words | Hook, proof, one objection handled, CTA | Solution-aware or product-aware audiences |

Word counts are an estimate, not a guarantee. Read every script aloud with a timer
before you generate a single frame. A script that reads long on paper reads long on
camera.

Every spoken line and every on-screen word is a claim. Each one traces to the
product truth and stays inside the claims boundary in B01, section 1.

## 4. Step 3: the avatar and its rights (lectures 4.1 and 4.3)

Three checks happen before you touch the generator. Two more happen once the clip
exists.

| Kind of avatar | What is behind it | The rights question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stock avatar | A real actor who licensed their likeness to the platform | Does your plan's tier cover paid social use? |
| Custom avatar | A specific person, such as a founder or a real creator | Do you hold their documented, specific consent? Platforms increasingly require it uploaded, not just held on file |
| Synthetic avatar | No real person at all | Usually the simplest rights picture; it still needs disclosure |

| Before generating | Done? |
| --- | --- |
| Licence tier confirmed commercial for paid social, and screenshotted with the date | |
| Script word budget checked and read aloud | |
| Delivery style matched to the hook (not a generic upbeat default) | |

Some tools include commercial rights on every plan and others gate them behind a
higher tier. Check which one you are on before every campaign, not once when you
signed up.

| After generating | What to look for | Passed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lip-sync | Does the mouth match the audio at normal speed, not just paused | |
| Pacing against the script marks | Does the CTA land inside the format's real time budget | |
| Delivery tone | Does it match the hook | |
| Full watch, not a thumbnail | Problems in the middle third are the ones a preview frame misses | |

## 5. Step 4: the product shot (lecture 4.4)

A generated video has to hold accuracy across every frame, not just one.

| Shot | Generate or film | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avatar talking, no product in frame | Generate | Low risk once the licence and the clip checks pass |
| Generic background, setting, mood | Generate | Low stakes, nothing to misrepresent |
| Product being worn, used or demonstrated | Film it | Frame-to-frame accuracy risk is real, and colour or detail can drift by the last frame |

Film one real ten-second clip of the actual product and reuse it across every
variant. Check its first and last frame against the real product. One phone in the
workflow is a small cost against a product that quietly looks wrong for hundreds of
frames.

| First frame matches the real product? | |

## 6. Step 5: assembly, captions and the muted-watch test (lecture 4.5)

Watch the finished cut with the volume at zero, the way most of the audience will.

| Silence-proof check | Passed? |
| --- | --- |
| The hook's key words appear as text on screen in the first second | |
| The proof line is captioned, even over footage with no face | |
| The CTA text stays on screen long enough to read | |
| Captions run the exact script text throughout, not a summary or a paraphrase | |
| Every caption proofread against the product truth, the same as an image overlay | |
| The ad works with the sound off | |

A caption is still a claim. A typo in a caption carries the same risk as a typo
baked into a static.

## 9. Honest test

- Was the licence tier confirmed before generation, with evidence kept?
- Was every script read aloud against a timer?
- Did you watch each clip in full, not from a thumbnail?
- Is the product on screen your filmed clip, not a generated one?
- Did every ad pass the muted-watch test?
- Does every caption match the product truth word for word?
