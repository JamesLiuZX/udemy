A Canadian tribunal made an airline pay damages because its chatbot
invented a refund policy that didn't exist. The airline argued the
chatbot was "a separate legal entity." The tribunal didn't buy it.

That case is chapter 1 of a book I just published: Stop Guessing Whether
It Works.

It's for product managers, delivery leads, and analysts who are now
responsible for judging an AI feature with a QA process built for
software that behaves the same way twice. This one doesn't. Same input,
different output, so your acceptance criteria stop working and the
argument about whether something's "ready" gets settled by whoever's
most senior in the room instead of by evidence.

The book covers:
- Writing acceptance criteria as evaluation thresholds on a golden
  dataset, not pass/fail rules
- Pricing a feature before it ships, not on the invoice
- Scoping an agent with reliability math instead of optimism (95% per
  step is a coin flip at ten steps)
- A risk register that gets you through legal review instead of stalling
  in it
