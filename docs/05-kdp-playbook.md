# KDP playbook: the commercial layer for books/

> `books/` already has what most self-publishers never build: a craft system
> (scene openers, verified `[KEY-INSIGHT]` citations, page-design devices, em
> dash policy), a compliance layer (`books/docs/00-kdp-compliance.md`), and a
> real production pipeline (LaTeX interior + EPUB). Read `books/CLAUDE.md`
> first; nothing here repeats it.
>
> What the system does not yet have is a **commercial layer**: which title
> ships first, how a launch works, pricing, categories, covers, the review
> engine, and the author identity that ties seven titles together. That is
> this document. It is strategy, so unlike the craft docs it goes stale;
> re-check the market facts before each launch.

## 1. Review of the current portfolio

Seven titles, each with chapters 1 to 3 drafted, none publishable yet:

| Slug | Audience | Honest read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stop-guessing` | PMs/analysts accountable for AI features | The differentiated one: real expertise behind it (the course), a buyer with budget, almost no direct competition |
| `ai-employee` | Managers delegating work to AI | Strong framing, growing search demand, moderate competition |
| `reclaimed-hour` | General productivity | Biggest audience, thinnest moat, and self-help is the category KDP polices hardest |
| `ai-for-the-rest-of-us` | General public | Same trade-off as above, more saturated |
| `one-person-business` | Freelancers/solopreneurs | Good niche, decent search volume, mid competition |
| `resume-arms-race` | Recruiters | Sharpest premise of the seven, smallest audience |
| `ai-didnt-close-that-deal` | Sales leaders | Good premise, crowded sales-book shelf |

**The strategic problem is concentration, not quality.** The chapter prose
reviewed (e.g. `stop-guessing` ch.1) genuinely clears the trade-book bar:
scene opener, a disclosed composite, a real cited tribunal case, named
limits. But seven three-chapter manuscripts are worth less than one finished
book, for the same reason the course side holds that a half-finished section
is worth less than one finished lecture. And there is a second, sharper
reason not to run seven in parallel to the finish line:

**Publishing several AI-assisted advice books in a short window under one
new author account is the exact surface pattern KDP's mass-produced-AI
enforcement watches for.** The books are not that (the craft system is the
proof), but enforcement sees the pattern before it sees the craft, and
enforcement is account-level. Sequencing is therefore a compliance control,
not just a marketing choice.

### The sequencing decision

1. **Finish `stop-guessing` first, end to end.** It is the only title where
   the author's expertise is independently demonstrable (the course), which
   makes the AI-assisted declaration strongest, the bio honest, and the
   cross-sell flywheel real. It also has the clearest niche category path to
   a Best Seller flag.
2. **Launch it properly (§4), learn from 90 days of real data** (which
   channels moved copies, whether the course cross-sell converts, what the
   review velocity is), then pick title #2 with evidence instead of taste.
   Likely candidates on current reasoning: `ai-employee` or
   `one-person-business`.
3. **One launch every 6 to 8 weeks at most.** Never bulk-publish. The other
   five manuscripts are an asset shelf, not a queue with a deadline.

## 2. "NYT bestseller level" needs translating before it can be a target

The New York Times list is curated, weights sales through reporting physical
retailers, and in practice excludes almost all self-published KDP nonfiction
regardless of sales. A KDP-first book does not get on that list; that is a
distribution fact, not a quality judgement.

So split the ambition into its two real parts:

- **NYT-level craft:** achievable, and `books/CLAUDE.md` §4 already encodes
  most of it. The bar: a reader cannot tell the book from good trade-published
  business nonfiction.
- **Bestseller mechanics, translated to Amazon:**

| Target | Threshold | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amazon Best Seller flag | #1 in a well-chosen niche category | Social proof on the listing, screenshot for everything |
| Ratings | 50+ at 4.6 within 6 months of a title's launch | The browse-page conversion driver |
| Rank durability | Top 20k overall store rank sustained | Where organic Amazon recommendation traffic switches on |
| Referral test | A reader gifts it to their boss or team | The actual sales engine for business books, and the design target for the writing |

The referral test is why the field-guide length (the rebased 120 to 170
pages) is right: in business nonfiction, short books get finished, finished
books get reviewed, and reviewed books get gifted. Do not let any title
creep back toward the 280-page tome.

## 3. Per-title commercial checklist

Run this once per title, before its final chapters are drafted, because the
title/subtitle and category research occasionally feed back into the
manuscript's framing.

| Lever | Decision rule |
| --- | --- |
| Title validation | Check the working title and subtitle phrases against Amazon search autocomplete and the top 20 results for the target queries. The subtitle carries the keywords (all seven current subtitles already do this well); the test is whether the niche's actual search phrases appear verbatim |
| Categories | 3 at upload: one winnable niche (the Best Seller flag comes from here), one mid-size, one aspirational. Verify current category sizes at launch time, not from memory |
| Keywords | All 7 KDP slots, phrase-level, no repetition of title/subtitle words (those are already indexed) |
| Ebook pricing | $0.99 launch week (velocity and rank compound), then $4.99, then $9.99 once 25+ ratings exist. The 70% royalty band is $2.99 to $9.99 |
| Paperback pricing | $18 to $24 against final page count and printing cost; also anchors the ebook as cheap |
| KDP Select / KU | Enrol for the first 90 days (free-day and countdown promos help launch), reassess per title; B2B titles gain little from KU read-through after launch |
| A+ content | Build from day one; reuse the interior's figures and pull-quote design language. Almost no self-published business book uses this surface well |
| ISBN | Own ISBNs if the plan is ever-wider distribution; KDP's free one locks the imprint field |

**Covers are the one asset the design system cannot produce.** Commission a
human designer (the $300 to $800 tier), brief them with the interior's
identity (Schola, ink blue, teaching-page restraint), and buy a **series
design**: seven titles sharing one visual system makes the author page read
as a publisher's list instead of a content farm's, which is precisely the
impression that matters given §1. Cover needs the locked page count for
spine width, so it starts after the manuscript freezes. No AI-generated
cover art, both for the same reason as the interiors and because it changes
the KDP disclosure answers.

## 4. Launch sequence and the review engine

Strictly inside Amazon's rules: never pay, never incentivise, never swap,
no friends-and-family reviews (filtered, sometimes penalised). What works
and is allowed:

1. **T-21 days:** ARC copies to readers recruited from the email list (and,
   for `stop-guessing`, offered to engaged course students). Nothing in
   exchange but the ask; ARC readers disclose the advance copy in their
   review.
2. **Launch week:** $0.99 ebook, announcement to the list, the LinkedIn/X
   assets from the growth engine, one Select free day mid-week if velocity
   stalls. Amazon Ads on from day one: an auto campaign at $5 to $10/day to
   harvest search terms, plus one exact-match campaign on the researched
   keywords. Kill anything above break-even ACOS after 30 days.
3. **The back-matter ask:** one sentence, immediately after the final
   chapter's last line, before the back matter proper. Highest-converting
   review surface a book has.
4. **T+90:** price to $9.99, reassess Select, decide title #2 from the data.

`growth/prompts/launch-calendar.md` expands this into a dated checklist with
pre-drafted copy; `growth/prompts/kdp-telemetry.md` tracks the aftermath.

## 5. Author identity across seven titles

Unresolved in the system today (`book.yaml` still says "Your Name"), and it
matters more than it looks:

- **One real author name across all titles**, one Author Central page, photo
  and bio consistent with the Udemy instructor profile. Cross-surface trust
  is checkable and buyers check it.
- **The bio must be honest per title.** For `stop-guessing` the credential is
  real and named (the course, the practitioner work). For titles outside the
  author's demonstrable expertise (`resume-arms-race`, `ai-didnt-close-that-deal`),
  the book's authority comes from its cited research, and the bio must not
  imply lived professional credentials that don't exist. This is both a KDP
  compliance posture and the difference between a book that survives expert
  readers and one that gets flagged by them in reviews.
- **Back matter cross-sells the shelf:** each book's final pages list the
  other published titles (only published ones) and the email-list lead
  magnet. The series cover design does the rest.

## 6. The loop with the course

- `stop-guessing` back matter: one page offering the companion artifact pack
  (rubric template, golden-set spec) via email signup on your own site,
  which then drips a course coupon. The book reaches readers who will never
  browse Udemy; the artifact pack converts the ones who want the hands-on
  version.
- Course bonus lecture (the one place Udemy permits promo): the book, one
  line, one link.
- Shared lexicon both ways: the named concepts (`the margin trap`,
  `fix the ruler`) appear identically in course and book, so a phrase heard
  in a meeting finds both products in one search.
