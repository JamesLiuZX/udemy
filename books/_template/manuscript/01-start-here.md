A chapter should open with a claim the reader can test against their own
life within the first page, not a throat-clearing paragraph about why the
topic matters in general. **Specificity** is the whole craft: a named
scenario beats a generic one, every time.

Consider a reader who has just been told, again, to "communicate more
proactively" without anyone telling them what that means in a specific
Tuesday-afternoon situation. The rest of this chapter would normally work
through exactly that situation, in enough detail that the reader could
recognise it as their own week.
